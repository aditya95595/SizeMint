ID photos and documents should not travel to a stranger's server just to get smaller. Here is how to hit 100KB entirely on your own device.

SPONSOR SPACE

When 100KB is the right target

One hundred kilobytes is the comfortable middle ground: job portals, ID document uploads, profile photos and blog images commonly land here. It is generous enough that a well-shot photo keeps visibly good quality, yet small enough to satisfy most upload limits and keep pages fast. If your form states a range ending at 100KB (Railway RRB's 50–100KB photo range, for example), target around 75KB to clear the ceiling with margin.

Your ID photo should not leave your device

Most online compressors work by uploading your photo to their server, processing it there, and sending it back. For a landscape that is harmless; for an ID photo, a signature or a document scan, it means your identity documents sit on hardware you do not control. Open your browser's developer tools (F12), switch to the Network tab, then compress a photo. Watch the request list: you will see no image upload traffic, because the file never leaves your machine. A note on EXIF data

Phone photos carry hidden EXIF metadata — camera model, timestamps, and sometimes GPS coordinates of where the photo was taken. Before uploading an ID photo anywhere, check what travels with it: on most systems you can right-click the file, open properties/details, and see the embedded data. Stripping EXIF is a separate step from compression, so verify your downloaded file the same way and remove location data before submitting identity documents.

If the photo still looks soft

At 100KB the target is rarely the problem — the source usually is. Photos taken in dim light, heavy digital zoom, or aggressive phone "beauty" filters compress poorly because the encoder wastes bytes on noise. Retake in daylight with zoom at 1x. If the file is a PNG screenshot of a photo, converting to JPG first typically doubles the usable quality at the same size.

100KB photo FAQs

How do I reduce a photo to 100KB without losing quality?

Enter 100 KB as an exact target instead of lowering a quality slider blindly. At 100KB a good source photo keeps visibly clean quality for ID and web use.

How do I know my photo was not uploaded?

Open the Network tab in your browser's developer tools and compress a photo. If no image data leaves your machine, the claim holds. SizeMint passes this test.

Does compression remove location data from my photo?

Not automatically — EXIF metadata is separate from pixel compression. Check the downloaded file's properties and strip location data before uploading identity documents.

Is 100KB enough for a job portal photo?

Yes for the file-size check, as long as dimensions, background and format also match the portal's notification. Size is one check among several.
