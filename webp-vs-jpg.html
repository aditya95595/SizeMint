WebP vs JPG: which compresses better?

Short answer: WebP, usually by a clear margin. Long answer: the form you are uploading to may not care — and that decides for you.

File size: WebP wins

At the same visual quality, WebP is typically 25–35% smaller than JPG for photographs. That is not a rounding error — it is the difference between hitting a 100KB target comfortably and fighting for every byte. The gap comes from WebP's more modern encoding: better prediction, better handling of smooth gradients. The advantage narrows at very high quality settings and on simple graphics, where there is less redundancy to exploit.

Quality: WebP wins, with one asterisk

At the same file size, WebP generally looks cleaner — fewer block artifacts, smoother gradients. The asterisk: at very small sizes both formats degrade, and WebP can smear fine text slightly differently than JPG. Judge at the size the image will actually be displayed, not zoomed in.

Transparency: WebP wins outright

JPG has no transparency at all. WebP supports transparency in both lossy and lossless modes, which makes it a strong PNG replacement for web graphics at a fraction of the size. If you are currently shipping transparent PNGs on the web, WebP is the single biggest size win available.

Compatibility: JPG wins, and this is the whole game

Every browser made in the last decade reads WebP. But upload portals are not browsers — many government and exam systems, older CMS platforms, and legacy desktop tools accept JPG only and reject WebP outright. A 40KB WebP against a portal that demands JPG is a rejected upload, full stop. When a form names a format, the comparison is over: use the format it names.

The practical verdict

Websites and general sharing: WebP, unless you need maximum compatibility. Forms and portals: whatever the instructions say — usually JPG. Questions, answered

Which is smaller, WebP or JPG?

At the same visual quality, WebP is usually 25–35% smaller than JPG for photographs.

Should I use WebP for exam forms and government portals?

Only if the form explicitly accepts it. Many older portals accept JPG only — a smaller file in the wrong format still fails.

Does WebP support transparency?

Yes, in both lossy and lossless modes — a strong, much smaller replacement for transparent PNGs on the web.

Is WebP supported everywhere now?

In browsers, yes. The remaining gaps are legacy upload portals and old desktop software — exactly where size limits are strictest.
