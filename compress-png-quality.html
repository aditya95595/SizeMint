How to compress PNG without losing quality.

PNG has two ways to get smaller: lossless optimization that changes nothing, and palette quantization that looks identical on graphics. Here is how both work and when to use each.

SPONSOR SPACE

Why PNG files are so big

PNG stores every pixel exactly — that is its job. For a logo with flat colors, "exactly" costs little. For a photograph with millions of subtly different pixels, "exactly" can cost 5–10MB where a JPG would need 300KB. Before optimizing a PNG, ask whether the image should be a PNG at all: photographs almost never should be. If it is a photo, convert it to WebP or JPG first — that single decision dwarfs every optimization below.

Method 1: lossless optimization (changes nothing)

Lossless optimizers rewrite the PNG's internal encoding more efficiently — better filtering, better deflate settings, stripped metadata — without touching a single pixel. Think of it as zipping the file smarter. Typical savings are 10–40%, the image is bit-for-bit identical, and there is no reason not to do it. This is always the first step.

Method 2: palette quantization (looks identical on graphics)

Quantization reduces the color palette — say, from 16 million colors to 256. That sounds drastic, but a logo or UI screenshot rarely uses more than a few hundred distinct colors, so the result is visually identical while the file can shrink 60–80%. This is technically lossy, which is why the method matters: use it on graphics, icons, charts and screenshots — never on photographs or gradients, where the missing colors show up as visible banding.

When PNG is the right choice

Use PNG when you need transparency (logos, overlays, watermarks), pixel-perfect screenshots for documentation, or line art where JPG's smudging around sharp edges would show. For everything else, pick the format by content: photos go to JPG or WebP, simple graphics stay PNG and get quantized. Choosing the right format up front beats any amount of squeezing the wrong one.

What to realistically expect

A logo or icon: lossless optimization alone often cuts 20–40%; with quantization, 60–80% smaller at visually identical quality is normal. A full-screen screenshot: expect 40–70% with careful settings. A photograph saved as PNG: convert it to JPG or WebP instead — no PNG technique will beat simply using the right format, and you will typically land at one-tenth the size.

Frequently asked questions

Does compressing a PNG reduce its quality?

Lossless optimization never does — the pixels are identical. Quantization is technically lossy but visually lossless on graphics with limited colors. On photographs, both the wrong method and visible damage.

Why is my PNG still huge after compressing?

Almost always because it is a photograph (or a very large image) in a format built for exactness. Convert photos to JPG or WebP. If it is a graphic, check the dimensions — a 4000px-wide logo carries 16× the pixels of a 1000px one.

Should I use PNG or JPG for screenshots?

PNG for documentation and anything with text — JPG smudges sharp edges. If the screenshot is mostly a photograph (a video still, a game capture), JPG or WebP at high quality is fine and far smaller.

Does SizeMint keep PNG transparency?

Yes — transparent pixels stay transparent through compression. Verify against a dark background after downloading, as described above.
