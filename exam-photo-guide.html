Compress photo for exam forms: SSC, UPSC, IBPS and more.

Government exam portals reject photos that miss their exact size limits. Here are the limits that matter and how to hit them on the first try.

Worth knowing before you start

We built the target-size workflow in SizeMint for exactly this situation: a phone photo, a portal with a hard KB limit, and a deadline. What follows is the short version of what we would tell a friend filling in the form the night before.

In practice, the photo fails more often than the signature. Signatures are simple black-on-white strokes and compress easily; photos carry faces, backgrounds and shadows. If you are short on time, get the photo right first.

Keep the compressed file until the application is fully submitted. If a later step asks you to re-upload, redoing the compression from scratch under time pressure is how mistakes happen.

Aim for the middle of the stated range. For a 20–50KB photo, a 35KB file passes the same check as a 49KB one — and it keeps noticeably better quality than a file squeezed down to the minimum.

Why exam forms reject your photo

Exam portals do not ask for "good quality". They ask for a photo between two numbers — for example, 20KB to 50KB — in JPG format, at roughly passport dimensions. A phone photo is usually 2–6MB, which is 40 to 300 times too large. The portal checks the file before a human ever sees it, so an oversized upload fails instantly with an "invalid photo size" error.

There is a second trap: some portals also reject photos that are too small. If your file falls below the minimum, the form assumes it lacks usable detail. Aim for the middle of the stated range, not the edges.

Photo and signature limits by exam

Always check your official notification — portals update specs every cycle. These are the commonly published ranges:

Photo

SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD): 20KB–50KB, JPG, around 3.5 × 4.5 cm. UPSC: 20KB–300KB, JPG. IBPS (PO, Clerk, RRB): 20KB–80KB, JPG. NEET: 10KB–200KB, JPG. Railway RRB: 50KB–100KB, JPG.

Signature

SSC and IBPS: 10KB–20KB, JPG, roughly 140 × 60 pixels, black ink on plain white paper. Crop tightly — extra white margin wastes bytes that could carry ink detail.

Compress to the exact limit in SizeMint

1. Start from the required range

Read your notification and note the minimum, maximum, format and any pixel dimensions. Pick a target near the middle of the KB range — for a 20–50KB photo, target 35KB.

2. Upload and set the target

Open the SizeMint compressor, drop in your photo, and enter the target size. The compressor searches for the highest quality that fits, so you do not have to guess a quality number.

3. Check faces and ink lines

Zoom into the result. Faces should stay recognizable and signature strokes should stay dark. If lines look grey and thin, the source photo was probably taken in poor light — retake it in daylight rather than compressing harder.

4. Confirm the file before uploading

Check the downloaded file's actual size in your file manager and confirm it sits inside the required range. Keep the original photo so you can re-export if a different form asks for a different limit.

Questions, answered

Will 20KB be enough for my exam form?

Only if your notification says so. SSC asks for 20–50KB for photos, which makes 20KB the floor rather than the goal — target around 35KB instead. Limits change every cycle, so always read your own notification; portals reject files outside the stated range in either direction.

Why does my portal reject a photo that looks fine?

Because the portal checks numbers, not looks. It validates file size, format and sometimes dimensions before any human sees the image. A 2MB phone photo fails a 50KB limit no matter how good it looks — the fix is compression, not a better camera.

Should I compress my signature the same way as my photo?

The steps are the same but the target differs: signatures usually need 10–20KB. Crop the signature tightly first, because white margins waste bytes. Black ink on plain white paper compresses extremely well, so signatures rarely cause trouble.

Can I reuse one compressed photo for multiple forms?

Only if the limits match. Different exams publish different ranges, and a file made for one portal can fail another. Keep your original photo and export a fresh copy per form — it takes seconds in SizeMint.
