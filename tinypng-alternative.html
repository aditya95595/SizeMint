SizeMint guides

A TinyPNG alternative that stays in your browser.

TinyPNG is good at what it does. But it uploads your images, caps you at 20 at a time, and never targets an exact size. Here is the alternative for people who want none of that.

What is actually different

No uploads. TinyPNG sends your images to its servers for processing. SizeMint compresses entirely in your browser — open your Network tab while compressing and watch nothing leave. For exam documents, ID photos, and anything personal, that difference matters.

Exact target sizes. TinyPNG applies smart compression and reports whatever size results. SizeMint works the other way: you name the target — 20KB, 100KB, any custom value — and it searches encodings to fit under it. When a form demands "under 50KB", that is the feature that matters.

No caps. TinyPNG's free tier limits you to 5MB per image, 20 images per batch, and 500 API compressions a month. SizeMint has no account, no batch limits, no API quota — because there is no server doing the work.

The honest part

TinyPNG's compression quality is genuinely good, and for quick one-off PNG crushes it remains a fine tool. SizeMint is not "better" at everything — it is better at three specific things: hitting exact byte targets, keeping your images private, and working without accounts or limits. If you need a Photoshop plugin or a CDN API, TinyPNG's paid tiers are the right call. If you need a 20KB exam photo without uploading your face to a server, you are reading the right page.

Switching takes thirty seconds

There is nothing to install and no account to create. Open the SizeMint compressor, drop in the same images you would feed TinyPNG, type your target size, and download the results. The workflow you already know, minus the upload queue.

Questions, answered

Is SizeMint really free?

Yes. No account, no image limits, no paywalled API — the compressor runs entirely in your browser.

Does SizeMint upload my images like TinyPNG does?

No. Everything is processed locally. Verify it yourself in your browser's Network tab while compressing.

Can SizeMint hit an exact file size like 20KB?

Yes — that is the core difference. Name the target and the compressor searches encodings to fit under it. Try compressing to 20KB or to 100KB.

What are TinyPNG's limits?

5MB per image, 20 images per batch on the free web tool, and 500 free API compressions a month. SizeMint has none of these caps.
