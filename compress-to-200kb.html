Compress image to 200KB, exactly.

200KB is the comfortable limit: enough budget to keep real quality. Here is how to hit it exactly without wasting the headroom.

Worth knowing before you start

200KB rewards good decisions. Unlike 20KB, this budget lets you keep generous dimensions and high quality — the wins come from choosing the right format and a sensible width, not from aggressive squeezing.

Compare JPG and WebP. At 200KB you have room to generate both and pick the cleaner result. WebP usually wins on photographs; JPG remains the compatibility king. Our WebP vs JPG comparison has the details.

Do not downscale blindly. At this budget, keeping a 1600–2000px width is often fine. Shrink only to the size the destination actually displays.

When you need exactly 200KB

200KB shows up on document uploads, marketplace listings, CMS image fields, and forms that want "high quality but bounded". It is the limit where you stop fighting physics and start making choices — which is exactly why naming the target first still matters: 201KB fails a 200KB check the same mechanical way 2MB does.

Hit 200KB in four steps

1. Upload your image

Open the SizeMint compressor and drop in a JPG, PNG, WebP or AVIF file. Processing happens entirely in your browser — the image is never uploaded anywhere.

2. Enter 200 as the target

Type 200 KB as the target size. The compressor searches encodings automatically to find the best quality that fits under 200KB.

3. Compare and pick

Try both JPG and WebP output at 200KB and inspect faces, text and gradients at full size. Pick the cleaner file — at this budget the difference is visible.

4. Download and verify

Download the result and confirm the file size in your file manager before uploading it to the form. Keep your original — the 200KB file is a submission copy, not an archive.

What 200KB can and cannot do

At 200KB, photographs look genuinely excellent — clean detail, smooth gradients, readable text. This budget covers most web and document use with room to spare. What it cannot do is preserve print-resolution originals, but no form asking for 200KB wants those anyway. The main mistake at 200KB is over-compressing out of habit: if the tool reports it hit the target at high quality, trust the result and stop.

If the target cannot be reached

Rare at this budget. If it happens, the image is likely enormous (50+ megapixels) or a PNG with transparency you do not need. Set a maximum width matching the display size, drop transparency if the destination does not need it, and try again.

Questions, answered

Is 200KB enough for high quality?

Yes — for screen and document use, 200KB holds a high-quality photograph. Diminishing returns kick in fast above this for most destinations.

JPG or WebP at 200KB?

Generate both and compare. WebP typically edges out JPG on photos; JPG wins on compatibility. Our format comparison explains when each wins.

Should I still resize?

Only down to the display size. At 200KB there is no reason to crush a 2000px image to 800px — keep the pixels the destination can actually show.

What about PNG at 200KB?

Keep PNG for screenshots, graphics and anything needing transparency. For photographs, PNG at 200KB usually means heavy quality loss elsewhere — JPG or WebP will look better.
